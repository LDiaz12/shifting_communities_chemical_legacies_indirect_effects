## Abstract

Coral reefs are among the world's most diverse ecosystems and contribute substantially to ocean productivity and the carbon budget. Disturbances are shifting coral reefs to altered community states, yet how these new communities indirectly alter organismal physiology by modifying the local biogeochemical environment remains largely unknown. We investigated this biogeochemical feedback in a 25-day mesocosm experiment in Mo'orea, French Polynesia, exposing the common coral *Porites rus* to the biogeochemical environments generated by three distinct communities representative of recent states in this dynamic system (coral-, macroalgae-, and crustose coralline algae (CCA)-enriched) relative to a seawater-only control. While net community production (NCP) was similar among communities, net community calcification (NCC) was lowest in the macroalgae-enriched state. Community metabolism drove changes in the organic and inorganic carbon pool, with NCP increasing with both pH and dissolved organic carbon (DOC). These community-driven shifts affected corals at both the community and organismal level: elevated pH significantly enhanced both NCC and chlorophyll a (Chl a) content of the corals. The increase in Chl a cascaded to the holobiont, predicting positive changes in coral tissue biomass, gross photosynthesis, and respiration. This study reveals a critical indirect pathway of disturbance where reef community structure modulates local carbon chemistry, which in turn regulates the physiological performance of key reef-builders. The relationships found in this study highlight a feedback loop that can influence future ecosystem trajectories on coral reefs.

## Experimental Overview

A 25-day flow-through mesocosm experiment was conducted at the Richard B. Gump South Pacific Research Station, Mo'orea, French Polynesia (17.49°S, 149.83°W) in June 2024. Sixteen tanks (10 L volume; 22.5 × 22.5 cm benthic tile footprint) were assigned to one of four benthic community treatments (n = 4 tanks per treatment):

| Treatment | Description |
|---|---|
| `Control` | No benthic community (seawater only) |
| `Algae_Dom` | Macroalgae-dominated community |
| `Coral_Dom` | Coral-dominated community |
| `Rubble_Dom` | Crustose coralline algae (CCA) / rubble-dominated community |

Each tank housed six *Porites rus* coral fragments from 24 distinct genotypes (one fragment per genotype per treatment; 96 fragments total). Flow-through seawater entered from two inflow tables; tank water chemistry was measured at noon (12:00) and 9 PM (21:00) on each sampling date, with additional diel time points during a 24-hour intensive sampling event. Coral physiology was assessed at the end of the experiment.

| Column | Description | Units |

| `R` | Respiration rate | µmol O₂ cm⁻² h⁻¹ |
| `pH_mean` | Mean daytime tank pH (total scale), averaged across sampling dates | — |
| `DOC_mean` | Mean daytime tank dissolved organic carbon, averaged across sampling dates | µmol L⁻¹ |

| `TEMPINSITU` | In-situ seawater temperature | °C |

| `TA` | Total alkalinity | µmol kg⁻¹ |
| `Notes` | Field and laboratory QC annotations | — |

## Scripts (`scripts/`)

### `path_analysis_report_robust.qmd`
The primary analysis document. Renders to a self-contained HTML report. The document:

1. **Loads and processes** all raw data files: pH calibration, DIC calculation via `seacarb`, DOC merging, inflow-referenced delta variables (ΔTA, ΔDIC, ΔpH, ΔDOC), and community metabolic flux calculations (NEC, NEP via alkalinity and DIC anomaly methods)
2. **Applies outlier removal** using Tukey outer fences (IQR × 3) per variable per time block (noon and 9 PM treated separately)
3. **Tests treatment effects** on NCP, NCC, pH, and DOC using linear mixed-effects models (`lmer`; `TREATMENT + (1 | TANK_NUM)`) with Tukey post-hoc contrasts (`emmeans`)
4. **Fits a piecewise structural equation model** (`piecewiseSEM::psem`) at the coral fragment level testing the causal chain: community pH / DOC → Chl-a → endosymbiont density → tissue biomass / gross photosynthesis → respiration. All variables z-scored; genotype random intercept `(1 | GENOTYPE)` in all equations
5. **Estimates indirect effects** via the delta method (Sobel 1982) and compares them against bivariate mixed-effects regressions
6. **Generates all figures** (saved to `output/`)

Render with:
```r
quarto::quarto_render("scripts/path_analysis_report_robust.qmd")
```

---

### `clean_data.R`
Removes columns from the raw data files that are unused by the analysis, metadata, or BCO-DMO submission. Run once to produce the trimmed versions of the data files currently in `data/`. Not required for re-running the analysis (the `.qmd` reads the already-cleaned files).

---

### `export_bcodmo.R`
Reproduces the full carbonate chemistry processing pipeline and applies outlier rules to produce the two BCO-DMO submission files in `output/`. Outlier rules match those applied in `path_analysis_report_robust.qmd` exactly:

- **Coral physiology:** `mean_tissue_biomass > 30 mg cm⁻²` → `NA`
- **Carbonate chemistry:** IQR × 3 Tukey outer fences applied to `DOC`, `NEC`, `NEP`, `pH`, `TA` at noon; `DOC`, `NEC`, `NEP`, `TA` at 9 PM

Run with:
```r
source("scripts/export_bcodmo.R")
```
